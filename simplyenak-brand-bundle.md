## Brand Voice (read before writing)

- Write like texting a friend who loves food
- Warm, personal, knowledgeable — the Passionate Friend
- Show with specifics: names, years, dishes, stall numbers, neighborhoods
- Sell PRESENCE, never absence ("you'll taste the char kway teow..." not "you won't want to miss...")
- Short, direct sentences. "And", "But", "Plus" not "Moreover", "Furthermore"
- "Walk with us", "Join us", "Come eat with us"
- Use guest/friend/you — NOT customer, client, establishment, venue
- Use stall/shop/spot — NOT establishment or venue
- "Walk through", "visit", "eat at" — NOT "explore"
- Use the word "popular" sparingly — prefer specific dishes and places

### Banned Terms
- "authentic" — show it, don't claim it
- "unforgettable", "amazing", "incredible" — empty superlatives
- "curated", "bespoke" — industry-speak
- "We're not like other tour companies" — sell presence, not absence
- Em-dashes — banned entirely
- "embark on a journey", "immersive experience"
- "We're not X, we're Y"
- Vague claims without proof — use specifics (names, years, locations, dishes)

## Founder Story

Pauline started Simply Enak in 2011. Growing up in Malaysia, she spent countless mornings walking through wet markets with her mom. While her mom haggled for fish and picked vegetables, Pauline watched hawkers craft dishes passed down through generations. The sizzle of char kway teow on a screaming hot wok, the aromatic clouds of curry rising from street stalls, the careful art of folding roti canai.

When she started bringing friends from other countries around, she realised they were missing this world. They'd eat at air-conditioned restaurants with picture menus, never knowing that two streets away, a grandmother was making laksa the same way her mother taught her 60 years ago.

Simply Enak started because she wanted to share what she experienced as a child. Not the Malaysia you see in guidebooks, but the one she knows: the night markets, the family recipes, the vendors who remember your face and your favourite dish.

## Tour Descriptions

### 1. Kuala Lumpur Street Food
- **Duration:** 3.5 hours
- **Price:** RM 285
- **Location:** Kuala Lumpur (Chinatown)
- The essential KL food tour. Start where KL's food story begins: Petaling Street market. Walk through Chinatown's historic back lanes, sample 15-18 dishes across heritage stalls. Start at a family-run stall making yong tau foo since 1978 — stuffed tofu, bitter gourd, and eggplant. Taste herbal tea, watch BBQ buns being hand-made, try KL's best curry laksa, and finish with a local dessert. Leave with a map of where to eat for the rest of your trip.
- **6 stops** including Petaling Street heritage zone, traditional medicine shop, and KL's best curry laksa

### 2. Flavours of Malaysia
- **Duration:** 4 hours
- **Price:** RM 289
- **Location:** Kuala Lumpur (Chow Kit + Kampung Baru)
- The most comprehensive tour. Deep dive into Malaysia's food culture across two very different neighborhoods. Start in Chow Kit, KL's oldest wet market — the wholesale market where restaurant owners shop before dawn. Walk through fish section, spice stalls (taste belacan and dried shrimp), watch aunties hand-making traditional biscuits. Then cross to Kampung Baru, a Malay village in the middle of the city, for nasi lemak, rendang, and kuih from stalls that have served the community for decades.
- **6 stops** including Jalan TAR, Chow Kit Market, Sin Hwa Bee Bakery, and Kampung Baru Malay village

### 3. Inside Pudu
- **Duration:** 3.5 hours
- **Price:** RM 289
- **Location:** Kuala Lumpur
- Walk through Pudu, the neighborhood where KL's Chinese immigrant families built a food culture so good no one has touched it for tourists. Meet at the last remaining gate of Pudu Jail (built by the British 1891-1895). Walk through streets that haven't changed in 50 years. Taste food from stalls that have been serving the same families for generations. This is the real KL that most visitors walk straight past.
- **Route:** Pudu Jail Gate → clan association temples → hidden hawker alleys

### 4. Secrets of KL — Nightlife, Street Art & Cocktails
- **Duration:** 4 hours
- **Price:** RM 359
- **Location:** Kuala Lumpur
- KL after dark is a different city. The heat fades, street art catches the last light, and hidden doors open to bars that were invisible at noon. Walk through Chinatown's backstreets as murals emerge in evening light. Sunset drinks at a hidden rooftop bar with panoramic KL skyline views. Late-night meal at a spot only locals know. Three drinks, one late-night meal, four hours.
- **5 stops:** Pasar Seni MRT → street art in Chinatown → rooftop sunset drinks → secret speakeasy → late-night meal

### 5. Penang Street Food
- **Duration:** 3.5 hours
- **Price:** RM 289
- **Location:** Penang (George Town)
- Penang is Malaysia's food capital. Eat your way through the street food the island is famous for. Walk past colonial monuments and step back to when Penang was formed as a British settlement in 1786. At Chowrasta Market, try kopitiam coffee and watch the morning trade. Taste dishes you'll only find in Penang — char kway teow with local prawns, Penang laksa with its signature sour broth, cendol with gula Melaka, nasi kandar. Have a light breakfast — you'll need the room.
- **Covers:** Chowrasta Market, Kimberley Street, Armenian Street heritage area

### 6. Georgetown's Best Hawkers and the Durian Strip
- **Duration:** 4 hours
- **Price:** RM 289
- **Location:** Penang (George Town)
- George Town comes alive at sunset. Start at an open-air hawker centre where cooking happens right in front of you — Hokkien Mee tossed over open flames until the prawn broth turns smoky and dark, fried carrot cake sticky and caramelised, loh bak wrapped in bean curd skin. Walk one of the world's most beautiful streets — a shophouse-lined street named after the first president of the Penang Chinese Chamber of Commerce. Finish at the Jalan Macalister durian strip, where you can try durian in season. Your guide shares stories about Penang history, the different Chinese dialect groups, and how each dish reflects the island's multicultural past.
- **6 stops:** Presgrave Hawker Centre → heritage street → clan jetties → Jalan Macalister durian strip

### 7. Navigate Malaysia's Vegetarian Scene Like a Local
- **Duration:** 4 hours
- **Price:** RM 450 (private tour)
- **Location:** Kuala Lumpur
- Finding vegetarian food in Malaysia is challenging as a tourist. Most restaurants don't mark options, and many dishes that look vegetarian contain hidden animal products (oyster sauce, shrimp paste). This private tour takes you to the spots locals actually eat at. 15+ tastings across Indian, Chinese, and Buddhist traditions. No hidden animal products. Covers a Buddhist monastery canteen, Little India banana leaf rice, and KL's best vegetarian Chinese stalls.
- **5 stops** across Buddhist, Chinese, Indian vegetarian traditions

### 8. Georgetown's Best Hawkers and the Durian Strip (afternoon variant)
- **Duration:** 4 hours
- **Price:** RM 289
- **Location:** Penang (George Town)
- Slightly different afternoon start than the sunset version. Start at a popiah stall in the UNESCO zone — fresh spring rolls with jicama, carrot, tofu, bean sprouts, and chilli. Walk through Little India as jewellery shops wind down, try vadai (crunchy lentil fritters) with coconut chutney made from a three-generation recipe. Continue through Armenian Street heritage area, taste cendol and apom from street vendors in front of pre-war shophouses. End at a clan jetty where a third-generation resident serves home-cooked Peranakan food: buah keluak, inchi kabin, and acar.
- **5 stops:** Popiah stall → Little India → Armenian Street → clan jetty Peranakan dinner

## The Problem We Solve For Travelers

Malaysia's best food doesn't have signposts in English. The stalls that serve transcendent char kway teow are hidden in wet markets, down alleyways, and in neighborhoods tourists don't know exist. Most visitors eat at food courts and air-conditioned restaurants with picture menus — good food, but not the real Malaysia.

Simply Enak bridges that gap. A local guide takes you to the spots they grew up eating at. You taste the dishes that define Malaysian cuisine, you hear the stories behind them, and you leave knowing where to eat for the rest of your trip (plus a WhatsApp contact for questions).
