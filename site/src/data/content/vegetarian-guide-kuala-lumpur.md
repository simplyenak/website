# The Vegetarian's Guide to Kuala Lumpur

Where to eat, what to order, and how to never get "accidental shrimp paste" again.

You're vegetarian, you're visiting KL, and you've been burned before by meals that said "vegetarian" but really weren't. Here's the good news: KL has some of the best vegetarian food in Asia — once you know where to look.

## Where Vegetarians Actually Eat Well in Kuala Lumpur

Kuala Lumpur has a secret that most tourists never discover: it's one of the best vegetarian cities in Southeast Asia. Not because of fancy plant-based restaurants (though those exist), but because vegetarian cooking has been part of the culture here for centuries.

Buddhist temples, Hindu eateries, and Chinese vegetarian traditions have all shaped KL's food scene — and most tourists walk right past the best spots.

## The Three Rules of Vegetarian Eating in KL

### 1. Brickfields is your best friend

KL's Little India, Brickfields, is where you'll find the highest concentration of vegetarian-friendly food in the city. The banana leaf rice stalls here have been feeding local office workers for decades.

### 2. Buddhist vegetarian is different from Western vegetarian

Chinese Buddhist vegetarian restaurants avoid onion, garlic, and leeks — not because they can't use them, but because of religious practice. These places are some of the best in KL. You'll find incredible mock meat dishes made from soy and mushroom.

### 3. Nasi kandar can work — if you know the system

The famous Malaysian nasi kandar (rice with curries) is usually a minefield for vegetarians because most stalls use shrimp paste. But there are specific stalls that serve vegetarian-friendly versions.

## The Honest Truth About "Vegetarian" in Malaysia

Here's what we've learned from 14 years of guiding vegetarian guests: Malaysian food is naturally more vegetarian-friendly than most cuisines, but you need to know what to ask for.

**Safe to order:** Roti canai (ask for no egg), banana leaf rice with vegetable curries, capati, thosai, vegetable fried rice (tell them no shrimp paste).

**Ask before ordering:** Nasi lemak (sambal often contains shrimp paste), char kway teow (uses lard traditionally), laksa (contains shrimp paste).
