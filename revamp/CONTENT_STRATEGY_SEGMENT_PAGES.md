## Google Helpful Content Requirements

Per Google's "Helpful Content System" and Quality Rater Guidelines, every page must demonstrate:

### 1. Experience (First-Hand Knowledge)
Content must show **direct experience** with the topic. Not research. Not AI generation. Real, lived expertise.

**For Simply Enak:**
- "After 14 years of guiding vegetarians through KL, we know the hidden meat problem..."
- "Pak Din has been fanning charcoal fires for 40 years. His secret isn't just the marinade..."
- "We've taken 2,000+ vegans through Brickfields. Here's what actually works..."

### 2. Expertise (Deep Subject Knowledge)
Content must demonstrate **understanding beyond surface level**.

**For Simply Enak:**
- Explain WHY Malaysian vegetarianism is different (Buddhist vs Hindu vs Jain)
- Explain WHY halal certification works differently in Malaysia vs Middle East
- Explain WHY gluten-free is challenging (soy sauce in everything, cross-contamination at hawker stalls)

### 3. Authoritativeness (Recognition by Others)
Content must show **external validation** of expertise.

**For Simply Enak:**
- "Featured in National Geographic, Lonely Planet, The New York Times"
- "Recommended by concierge at Four Seasons, Mandarin Oriental, St. Regis"
- "5,000+ guests, 500+ TripAdvisor reviews, 14 years operating"
- "President of Les Clefs d'Or Malaysia recommends our vegetarian routes"

### 4. Trustworthiness (Honesty & Transparency)
Content must be **honest about limitations and challenges**.

**For Simply Enak:**
- "Strict Jain requirements need 48-hour notice — we work with dedicated kitchens"
- "Cross-contamination is real at hawker stalls. We know which vendors keep separate woks"
- "Not every dish can be made vegan. We'll tell you which ones to skip"

## Content Structure for Every Segment Page

Each page must have these sections minimum (800-1500 words total):

### 1. Hero Section (100-150 words)
- **Hook**: Address the specific user's anxiety/fear
- **Promise**: What they'll experience that's unique to Malaysia
- **Credibility**: One trust signal specific to this segment

### 2. The Challenge (200-300 words)
- What's hard about this dietary/travel need IN MALAYSIA specifically
- Common mistakes tourists make
- Why generic advice doesn't work here

### 3. What Makes Malaysia Unique (200-300 words)
- Cultural context specific to this segment
- Historical/culinary background
- What surprised previous guests

### 4. Our Approach (200-300 words)
- How we solve the challenge (specific vendors, techniques, relationships)
- First-hand stories from guides
- Specific examples, not generic claims

### 5. Recommended Tours (cross-link block)
- 2-4 specific bookable tours with WHY each fits this segment
- Price range, duration, what to expect
- Clear CTAs

### 6. Practical Tips (100-200 words)
- 3-5 actionable tips for this segment traveler
- What to ask for, what to avoid, how to communicate

### 7. FAQ (3-5 questions)
- Real questions this segment asks
- Honest answers, including limitations

**User Intent:** "Can I find good vegetarian food in Malaysia? Is it safe? What should I know?"

**Hero:**
> Malaysia is one of the world's most vegetarian-friendly countries — if you know where to look. Buddhist Chinese temples serve mock meat so convincing you'll check twice. **The Challenge:**
- "Vegetarian" means different things in Malaysia
- Hidden meat: belacan (shrimp paste), ikan bilis (anchovies), lard
- Cross-contamination at mixed hawker stalls
- Language barriers: "Sayur" means vegetables but staff may not understand strict requirements

**What Makes Malaysia Unique:**
- Buddhist vegetarian tradition (no onion/garlic either)
- Indian vegetarian vs Chinese vegetarian differences
- Nyonya cuisine has vegetarian versions of everything
- Hawker culture: made-to-order means customization is possible

**Our Approach:**
- We pre-walk every route with vegetarian requirements
- We know which vendors have separate cooking stations
- Our guides speak Malay, Mandarin, Cantonese, Tamil — they can explain exactly what you need
- We have relationships with temple kitchens for strict requirements

**Recommended Tours:**
- Eat & Drink George Town (Penang) — Buddhist vegetarian options at every stop
- KL Street Food — Our guide knows which stalls use vegetable oil only
- Little India Brickfields — 100% vegetarian route available

**Tips:**
1. Learn "Saya vegetarian" (I am vegetarian) — but also learn "Tidak makan daging, ikan, atau udang"
2. Specify "no belacan" — shrimp paste is the hidden killer
3. Buddhist temples are your safest bet for strict vegetarian
4. Indian restaurants understand better than Chinese hawkers

**FAQ:**
Q: Will I get enough protein?  
A: Absolutely. Tofu, tempeh, lentils, chickpeas are staples.

Q: Can I eat at hawker stalls?  
A: Yes, but only ones our guides have verified. Cross-contamination is real.

Q: What about vegan?

**The Challenge:**

**What Makes Malaysia Unique:**
- Rice is the staple starch — most meals are rice-based
- Rice noodles (kuey teow, mee hoon, laksa) are naturally gluten-free
- Coconut aminos available at some health-conscious vendors
- Traditional Malay cooking uses less soy sauce than Chinese

**Our Approach:**
- We identify vendors who use tamari or coconut aminos
- We know which stalls have separate woks for rice noodles
- Our guides can explain "saya allergic kepada gluten" in Malay, Mandarin, Tamil
- We carry gluten-free translation cards

**Recommended Tours:**
- Malay Heritage — Naturally gluten-free Malay dishes
- Penang Street Food — Rice noodle dishes curated
- Custom tour — We can design a 100% gluten-free route

**Tips:**
1. Rice noodles (kuey teow) are safe — wheat noodles (mee) are not
2. Satay sauce often contains wheat — ask first
3. Roti canai is wheat-based — skip it
4. Bring a translation card — "gluten" is not widely understood

## Content Quality Checklist

Every page must pass these checks before publishing:

- [ ] **First-hand experience**: Contains specific details only a local guide would know
- [ ] **Expertise depth**: Explains WHY, not just WHAT
- [ ] **Authoritativeness**: References real media, real partnerships, real numbers
- [ ] **Trustworthiness**: Honest about limitations and challenges
- [ ] **Originality**: Not generic travel advice — Malaysia-specific insights
- [ ] **Comprehensiveness**: Answers all obvious questions a visitor would have
- [ ] **Actionability**: Specific tips, vendor names, dish names, neighborhoods
- [ ] **Cross-links**: Recommends 2-4 specific bookable tours with clear reasoning
- [ ] **FAQ**: Addresses real concerns (safety, dietary, logistics)
- [ ] **Word count**: Minimum 800 words, ideally 1200-1500
