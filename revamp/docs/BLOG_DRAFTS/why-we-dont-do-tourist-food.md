## Hero Section (100 words)

### Tourist Food Is Killing Malaysian Food Culture

**You know that "Malaysian food" you get at tourist restaurants? The mild rendang. The sanitized nasi lemak. The food that tastes like it was designed by committee?**

**That's not Malaysian food. That's tourist food. And we don't do it.**

Here's the thing: tourist food isn't bad because it's made for tourists. It's bad because it's designed to be inoffensive. To not challenge. To not offend sensitive palates. To be... fine.

Malaysian food isn't fine. Malaysian food is bold, complex, sometimes challenging, always memorable. And that's what we show our guests.

## What Is Tourist Food? (200 words)

### The Characteristics

**Tourist food has telltale signs:**

**1. Mild Flavors**
- Less chili (can't handle the heat)
- Less spice complexity (one-note flavors)
- Less salt (bland by Malaysian standards)
- Less everything that makes Malaysian food Malaysian

**2. Sanitized Ingredients**
- No belacan (shrimp paste smells too strong)
- No petai (stink beans are too... pungent)
- No offal (too adventurous)
- No anything that might challenge Western palates

**3. Generic Presentation**
- Served on white plates (not banana leaves)
- Air-conditioned restaurants (not hawker stalls)
- Forks and spoons (not hands)
- Clean, sterile, boring

**4. High Prices**
- RM 25-40 per dish (hawker: RM 8-15)
- Tourist markup (because tourists don't know better)
- Nice ambiance (you're paying for the AC, not the food)

### Where You Find Tourist Food

**Tourist food lives in:**
- Hotel restaurants
- Airport "Malaysian cuisine" stalls
- Shopping mall "food courts"
- Places with English-only menus
- Places with photos of every dish
- Places that serve "Malaysian buffet"

## What We Do Instead (250 words)

### We Take You Where Locals Eat

**Our philosophy:** If we wouldn't eat there, we don't take guests there.

**What this means:**

**1. Hawker Stalls Over Restaurants**
- 40-year-old auntie making laksa the same way for four decades
- Plastic stools and ceiling fans (not white tablecloths)
- RM 8-12 per dish (not RM 25-40)
- Flavor that punches you in the face (in the best way)

**2. Bold Flavors**
- Chili that makes you sweat (but you can't stop eating)
- Spice complexity (you taste something new with each bite)
- Salt levels that make sense (Malaysian food is seasoned properly)
- Flavors that challenge and reward

**3. Real Ingredients**
- Belacan (yes, it smells strong — that's the point)
- Petai (stink beans are delicious, fight us)
- Offal (for adventurous eaters)
- Everything that makes Malaysian food Malaysian

**4. Local Crowds**
- If the place is full of tourists, we don't go there
- If the place is full of locals, we do go there
- Simple metric, never wrong

## Why Tourist Food Is a Problem (200 words)

### It Erases Culture

**When you serve tourist food, you're not just serving bad food. You're erasing culture.**

**Here's how:**

**1. Recipes Get Watered Down**
- Second generation takes over family stall
- "Let's make it milder for tourists"
- Original recipe is lost
- Next generation doesn't know the real thing

**2. Techniques Get Simplified**
- Spice paste used to take 4 hours to make
- Now it's store-bought (consistent, bland)
- Traditional technique dies
- No one knows how to make it the old way

**3. Ingredients Get Replaced**
- Fresh turmeric → turmeric powder
- Hand-pounded chili → blender (different texture)
- Charcoal fire → gas (no wok hei)
- Something is lost in translation

**4. Young People Don't Learn**
- Why learn 4-hour recipe when tourists accept 20-minute version?
- Economic incentive is gone
- Traditional knowledge dies with older generation
- Future generations eat tourist food thinking it's real

## FAQ (150 words)

### What if I can't handle spicy food?

We still take you to real places. We'll order non-spicy dishes, or ask for chili on the side. Real food doesn't have to be spicy — it has to be flavorful.

### What if I'm a picky eater?

Tell us upfront. We can't promise you'll love everything, but we'll find dishes that work for you. The goal is to expand your horizons, not make you miserable.

### Is tourist food always bad?

Not always. Sometimes it's just... inoffensive. Fine. Forgettable. The problem isn't that it's bad — it's that it could be so much better.

### Do you take dietary requirements seriously?

Yes. Vegetarian, vegan, halal, gluten-free, Jain — we accommodate all dietary needs. But we still take you to real places, not tourist restaurants.
