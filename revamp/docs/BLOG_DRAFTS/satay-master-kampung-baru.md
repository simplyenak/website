## How to Eat Satay Like a Local (150 words)

### The Right Way

**1. Use Your Right Hand**
- Left hand is considered unclean
- Pick up skewer with right hand
- Bite the meat off (don't pull with left hand)

**2. Dip Generously**
- Dip each piece in peanut sauce
- Don't be shy — the sauce is essential
- Some people pour sauce over ketupat (rice cakes)

**3. Order by the Stick**
- Satay is sold per stick
- Chicken: usually RM 0.80-1.20 per stick
- Beef/Lamb: usually RM 1.50-2.50 per stick
- Order 10-20 sticks per person (they're small)

**4. What to Order With It**
- **Ketupat:** Compressed rice cakes (soak up peanut sauce)
- **Nasi Impit:** Same as ketupat, different shape
- **Cucumber & Onion:** Refreshing contrast
- **Teh Tarik:** Perfect pairing

## FAQ (100 words)

### Is satay halal?

Yes, satay from Malay/Mamak stalls is halal. Look for halal certification or ask. Chinese satay may not be halal (they sometimes use pork).

### What's the difference between chicken and beef satay?

Chicken satay is sweeter, more tender. Beef satay is richer, chewier. Both use similar marinades but beef often has more spices.

### Can vegetarians eat satay?

Traditional satay is meat, but some stalls offer tofu or tempeh satay. Ask your guide — we can accommodate vegetarians on our tours.

### How many satay should I order?

Satay is small — 1-2 sticks per person is a taste, 10-20 sticks is a meal. We recommend ordering a mix of chicken and beef.
