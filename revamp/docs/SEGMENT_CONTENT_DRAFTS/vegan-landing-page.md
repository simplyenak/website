## Understanding Vegan in Malaysia (250 words)

### Three Vegan Traditions

**Chinese Buddhist Vegetarian (Often Vegan)**
- No meat, no egg, no dairy
- No onion, garlic, chives (the "five pungent roots")
- Mock meats made from gluten, soy, mushrooms
- Found in Buddhist temple canteens, 素食 (sù shí) stalls
- Often 100% vegan by default

**Indian Vegetarian (Can Be Made Vegan)**
- No meat, but often includes dairy (ghee, yogurt, paneer)
- Can request vegan modifications
- Found in Little India, Indian restaurants
- Common in KL's Brickfields

**Modern Vegan Cafes (Growing Trend)**
- Western-style vegan food (burgers, smoothie bowls)
- Plant-based desserts, vegan coffee
- Found in trendy neighborhoods (Bangsar, Mont Kiara)
- Popular with younger Malaysians

### Hidden Animal Products

**Egg Noodles**
- Mee (yellow noodles) often contains egg
- Ask for bee hoon (rice vermicelli) instead

**Ghee (Clarified Butter)**
- Used in some Indian dishes
- Request oil instead

**Condensed Milk**
- In teh tarik, kopi, desserts
- Request soy milk or black coffee/tea

**Shrimp Paste (Belacan)**
- In sambal, Malay dishes
- Vegetarian versions exist

**How We Handle This:**
We brief every vendor before the tour. We tell them you're vegan and what that means for you. We know which stalls use plant-based oils, which can skip the ghee, which have vegan versions of traditional dishes.

## FAQ (200 words)

### Is Malaysia good for vegans?

Yes, increasingly so. Malaysia has a strong Chinese Buddhist vegetarian tradition that's often accidentally vegan (no meat, no egg, no dairy). Plus, modern vegan cafes are popping up everywhere in KL and Penang. The variety is enormous, from traditional hawker food to modern plant-based cuisine. Once you know where to look, you'll eat very well.

### What's the difference between Buddhist vegetarian and vegan?

Chinese Buddhist vegetarian food excludes meat, eggs, dairy, AND the "five pungent roots" (onion, garlic, chives, spring onion, leeks). This makes it stricter than Western veganism in some ways (no onion/garlic) but similar in others (no animal products). Most Buddhist vegetarian stalls are 100% vegan by default.

### Can Simply Enak accommodate vegan guests?

### What's the biggest challenge for vegans in Malaysia?

Hidden animal products are the biggest challenge. Egg noodles, ghee in Indian food, condensed milk in drinks, shrimp paste in sambal — these catch many vegans off guard. But we know which stalls use plant-based ingredients, which can modify dishes, which are 100% vegan.
