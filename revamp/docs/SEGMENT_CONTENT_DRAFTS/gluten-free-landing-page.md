**Rice Noodles (Safe)**
- Bee hoon (rice vermicelli)
- Kuey teow (flat rice noodles)
- Laksa noodles (usually rice-based)
- Check: some "kuey teow" may contain wheat

**Naturally Safe Dishes**
- Satay (check marinade for soy sauce)
- Rendang (slow-cooked beef)
- Ikan bakar (grilled fish)
- Fresh fruits

### Hidden Gluten Sources

**Soy Sauce (Contains Wheat)**
- Used in most Chinese stir-fries
- Some Malay dishes use it too
- Ask for tamari or gluten-free soy sauce

**Oyster Sauce (Contains Wheat)**
- Default sauce for Chinese greens
- Some stir-fries use it for flavor
- Not always declared

**Wheat Noodles (Avoid)**
- Mee (yellow egg noodles)
- Mee pok (flat egg noodles)
- Some "kuey teow" varieties

We brief every vendor before the tour. We tell them you require gluten-free food and what that means for you. We know which stalls use tamari, which have dedicated gluten-free woks, which can modify dishes safely.

## FAQ (200 words)

### Is Malaysia good for gluten-free food?

Yes, surprisingly good. Malaysia's cuisine is heavily rice-based, which means many dishes are naturally gluten-free. Rice noodles, rice flour cakes, coconut-based curries — these are all naturally safe. The challenge is soy sauce, oyster sauce, and wheat noodles. But with a knowledgeable guide, you'll eat very well.

### What noodles are gluten-free in Malaysia?

Bee hoon (rice vermicelli) and kuey teow (flat rice noodles) are usually gluten-free. Laksa noodles are typically rice-based. Avoid mee (yellow egg noodles) and mee pok (flat egg noodles) — these contain wheat. When in doubt, ask: "Ini mee beras atau mee gandum?" (Is this rice noodles or wheat noodles?)

### Can Simply Enak accommodate gluten-free requirements?

Yes, completely. We've been accommodating gluten-free guests for 14 years, and we know exactly where to take you. Our tours include stalls that use tamari instead of soy sauce, have dedicated gluten-free woks, and understand cross-contamination risks. Just let us know when you book.

### What's the biggest challenge for celiac travelers in Malaysia?

Cross-contamination is the biggest challenge. Many stalls use the same woks for gluten-free and gluten-containing dishes. Soy sauce is added liberally and often not declared. But we know which stalls take gluten-free seriously, which have dedicated woks, which use gluten-free sauces.
