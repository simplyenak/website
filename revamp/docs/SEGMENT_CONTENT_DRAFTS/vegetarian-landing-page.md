## What Makes Malaysia Great for Vegetarians (250 words)

### Three Vegetarian Traditions in One Country

Malaysia sits at a cultural crossroads. Three distinct communities have shaped how vegetarian food looks and tastes here:

**Chinese Buddhist Vegetarian**
- No meat, no eggs, no dairy
- No onion, garlic, chives, leeks (the "five pungent roots")
- Mock meats made from gluten, soy, mushrooms
- Found in Buddhist temple canteens and 素食 (sù shí) stalls
- Common in KL's Chinatown, Penang's Georgetown

**Indian Hindu Vegetarian**
- No meat, but eggs and dairy often included
- Rich curries, dosas, thalis, banana leaf rice
- Found in Little India, mamak stalls, Indian restaurants
- Common in KL's Brickfields, Penang's Jalan Masjid Kapitan Keling

**Modern Vegan/Vegetarian Cafes**
- Western-style vegan cafes (growing trend)
- Plant-based burgers, smoothie bowls, vegan desserts
- Found in trendy neighborhoods (Bangsar, Mont Kiara in KL)
- Popular with younger Malaysians and expats

## The Hidden Ingredient Problem (200 words)

### What Looks Vegetarian But Isn't

Here's the catch: "Vegetarian" here often means "no meat" — fish sauce, shrimp paste, and egg still show up.

**Belacan (Shrimp Paste)**
- In almost every Malay vegetable dish
- Kangkung belacan (stir-fried water spinach) almost always contains it
- Sambal usually contains it
- You can't taste it — it's cooked into the base

**Fish Sauce**
- Used in Thai-influenced dishes
- Sometimes added to stir-fries for umami
- Not always declared on menus

**Oyster Sauce**
- Default sauce for Chinese-style greens
- In the glossy sauce on your gai lan
- Often added without anyone thinking to mention it

**Egg Noodles**
- Many "vegetarian" noodle dishes contain egg noodles
- Mee (noodles) often means egg noodles unless specified
- Bee hoon (rice vermicelli) is usually egg-free

## FAQ (200 words)

### Is Kuala Lumpur good for vegetarians?

Yes, genuinely. KL has one of the most developed vegetarian food scenes in Southeast Asia, rooted in Chinese Buddhist and South Indian Hindu traditions that go back generations. The variety is enormous, from temple canteens to hawker stalls to modern vegan cafes. Once you know how to read the signs, you'll eat very well.

### What does 素食 mean?

素食 (pronounced sù shí) is the Chinese term for Buddhist vegetarian food. It means the food excludes all meat, eggs, dairy, and the five pungent roots (onion, garlic, chives, spring onion, leeks). Look for this on signage outside stalls and restaurants — it's your clearest guide to strictly plant-based food in KL's Chinese areas.

### What's the biggest challenge for vegetarians in Malaysia?

The biggest challenge is that "vegetarian" means different things depending on the culture. Chinese Buddhist vegetarian is often stricter than Western veganism (no onion, no garlic). Indian vegetarian often includes eggs and dairy. And many dishes that look vegetarian contain belacan (shrimp paste) or fish sauce. We know which stalls are truly safe — that's what we're here for.
